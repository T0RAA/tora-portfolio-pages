The starting point

An AI driver that thinks well but answers late can still arrive too late. With the AIEA Lab’s AV Ethics team, I study how inference and actuation delays change outcomes in time-critical, dilemma-style driving scenarios.

What I contributed

Built the team’s CARLA experiment pipeline on the Nautilus Kubernetes cluster, grounded its physics in published sources, and ran controlled-delay, model-replay, and paired-ablation experiments for a paper the team is preparing.

How it works

CARLA 0.9.15 runs in synchronous, fixed-step mode inside Kubernetes Jobs with persistent storage. I calibrated braking at a 1 ms timestep against NHTSA and published-study targets, then varied one timing component at a time while holding each decision fixed, so any change in outcome traces back to the delay.

What came out of it

  • Ran a 144-trial controlled-delay grid across 3 speeds, 6 time-to-collision values, and 8 injected delays.
  • Replayed 18 measured LLM driving decisions with a 354 ms median decision latency.
  • Matched held-out braking targets within 0.8–11.6% (15% acceptance gate) and compiled a 22-parameter, source-cited table for the team’s simulator.
  • Produced 30 paired timing contrasts across seven driving actions with pedestrian actors. These are deterministic simulator observations, not real-world risk estimates.

The bigger possibility

Next, I’m exploring which road actors actually matter to a decision, using graph-based actor weighting and intervention tests. The aim is AI drivers whose reasons hold up when the world changes.
